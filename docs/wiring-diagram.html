ESP32 Controller Wiring

Every net below is read directly from this firmware's pin constants — Config.h, Core_04_Hardware.ino, Screen_06_PaymentOther.ino, Core_13_LTEModem.ino, Core_18_RTC.ino, Core_19_RFID.ino, Core_20_DropSensor.ino — not a generic ESP32 pinout. Reflects the 2-motor sanitary-napkin build (CFG_MOTOR_COUNT = 2): only M1/M2 are populated, M3/M4 stay reserved and unwired, and GPIO32/GPIO33 are each behind their own external dip switch rather than fixed to one job. The RFID reader and the LTE modem are fully wired and implemented in firmware but currently switched off in software (CFG_PAYMENT_RFID_AVAILABLE = false, CFG_LTE_ENABLED = false) — the code path exists, nothing was removed, so both re-enable with a flag flip and a reflash, no rewiring. Every ESP32 pin, including the unused and unusable ones, is accounted for in the complete pin map.

CFG_MOTOR_COUNT = 2 — initMotorPins() only ever touches the first two entries of MOTOR_PIN_POOL, so GPIO26/27/32/33 are never configured as outputs by firmware at this count; nothing collides if they're left completely unwired. Raising the count to 5 or 6 needs the matching dip switch(es) moved to their motor throw first (GPIO32 at 5, GPIO33 as well at 6), and a reflash either way — see COIN_POWER_PIN_FREE/COIN_PULSE_PIN_FREE in Screen_06_PaymentOther.ino for exactly how firmware backs off the coin subsystem as those pins get claimed.

Coin acceptor & power control

SignalESP32Note
Pulse inGPIO33behind dip switch A — throw A (default): counts RISING edges into the acceptor's pulse line, 5 ms debounce in the ISR. Throw B: reconnects this same pin to the M6 driver channel (CFG_MOTOR_COUNT = 6 + reflash).
Power ON/OFFGPIO32behind dip switch B — throw A (default): drives a relay/MOSFET that cuts the acceptor's own supply. Off (LOW-equiv per CFG_MOTOR_ACTIVE_HIGH) at boot; powered only while coinAcceptorListen(true) is active, i.e. while the cash screen is showing. Throw B: reconnects this same pin to the M5 driver channel (CFG_MOTOR_COUNT = 5 + reflash).
Pull-up3V3external 10kΩ — optional on GPIO33, which unlike GPIO34–39 has an internal pull-up available. Kept because the line was already wired this way.
GNDGNDcommon return

Pulse→value: 1 = Rs 1, 2 = Rs 2, 3–5 = Rs 5, ≥10 = Rs 10; 6–9 pulses are rejected as unrecognised. A burst is complete after 200 ms of silence (3 s safety cap).

Both dip switches are manual mode selectors, wired external to the ESP32 — firmware never reads either one's position. Moving a switch changes which physical device a GPIO reaches; the corresponding Config.h flag / CFG_MOTOR_COUNT has to be changed and reflashed to match, or the firmware will drive or listen on a pin wired somewhere it doesn't expect.

DS3231 recommended over a DS1307 — ±2ppm drift vs. ±20ppm, and it keeps time on its onboard CR2032 through a power cycle. Third fallback in the clock chain, after WiFi NTP and LTE NITZ/NTP — see Core_18_RTC.ino.

Spare — free for future use
GPIO36 (VP)in only—FreeAvoid. Sits directly beside EN on the header; a stray or adjacent connection that briefly pulls EN low holds the whole board in reset. Already hit once on this bench.
GPIO39 (VN)in only—FreeInput-only, no internal pull-up — same caveat as every GPIO34-39 pin (see GPIO35 above).
GPIO0in/out—FreeLast-resort spare for a new output — the only free output-capable pin left, and not broken out on every DevKit V1 variant. It is the boot-mode strap: held low at reset the chip enters flash-download instead of running. Anything wired here must leave it high at boot.
Unusable — never wire these
GPIO12in/out—Leave unconnectedFlash-voltage strap (MTDI), read at every reset and required LOW. An external connection here caused a real, reproducible upload failure on this project — which is why it is excluded from the motor pool by name.
GPIO6–GPIO11——Internal SPI flashBonded to the on-module flash die. Wiring anything here stops the board booting.

20 GPIOs wired to peripherals (2 of them dip-switched dual-role), 2 more on the USB console, 3 spare, 7 unusable. GPIO26/27 are counted as "wired to peripherals" in the sense that they're reserved motor-pool slots, but nothing is physically connected to them in this build; GPIO35 is counted the same way — claimed by the planned IR drop sensor, not yet actually wired on the bench. Physical header positions differ between 30-pin and 38-pin DevKit variants, so this table is ordered by function and GPIO number rather than by position on the strip — match the silkscreen labels on the board in front of you, not a pin count.
